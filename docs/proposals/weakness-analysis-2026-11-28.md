# Weakness analysis of the best policies (2026-11-28)

The user: "atm it doesn't creep, and often gets workers wrong (too many in goldmine), often loses heroes etc." and "items are rarely bought". This note puts numbers on those and on the other big gaps, the policy against the insane AI in the same games, and proposes fixes that do not repeat what failed before.

**Data.** (1) Evaluation rows of the strong policies (the soup, `fgself-267`'s EMAs at 27/54/81M, `fgself-268`'s EMA at 27M; bursts 264-268b and burst-soup): **996 games against the untaxed insane AI** (920 won, 69 lost, 7 tied: 0.924) and 617 at the handicap rungs (agent -25%: 342, 0.48 won; -50%: 275, 0.01). For some splits the plateau averages are added (252's, 260's, 261's, 267's SWAs: 1,583 untaxed games). (2) **171 per-step traces** of AI games by plateau-level policies (bursts 241-268 and soup: 98 untaxed, 90 won and 8 lost; 73 taxed, 13 won and 58 lost). (3) **4,752 mid-game states** (minutes 8-16) captured from training games against the real insane AI (`late-states.jsonl` of fgself-260, 261, 266-269), joined to their episode rows: 867 without a target, 3,885 with one. They hold each worker's job (gold or lumber: the agent's own assignment; for the AI its order and place). The AI plays at its double income throughout; the agent at a person's.

## Summary

**One syndrome first.** The policy has one strategy, a tier-1 rush with one hero, one unit type and one mine. Games under 15 minutes (80% of them) are won 0.993; 15-20 minutes (12%) 0.899; past 20 minutes (8%) **0.247**: 51 of the 69 losses and all 7 ties. At the -25% rung 72% of the games pass 15 minutes. In the long games every sign of the AI's development predicts a loss: the AI at tier 3 (0.06 won, n=65, against 0.93), with 10+ research levels (0/55), 3 heroes (0/54), 10+ creeps (0.06, n=64), 4+ air units (0/38, against 0.80). Winning the 20-minute games at the 15-20 minute rate would add ~5 points against the untaxed AI and much more on the rungs. The weaknesses below are the parts of that syndrome the policy controls, ranked by my estimate of cost (gap size, how far upstream, how clearly the data ties it to losing).

## 1. Economy: workers on the wrong job

**Evidence.**
- **Gold is capped by one mine; the extra workers add nothing.** Gold a minute by workers trained (games of 8+ min): human 487 (0-3 trained), 509 (4-6), 519 (7-9), 497 (10-13), 506 (14+); orc 533-552; undead 437-509; night elf 544-556. Lumber barely moves with them (human 37 to 52, orc 54 to 87). The AI (double income): human 655 gold / 284 lumber, orc 825 / 272, undead 807 / 108, night elf 1,091 / 382.
- **Where the workers are** (captured states at ~11 min, the agent's own assignment). Without a target: human 8.9 workers, 7.3 on gold, **1.1 on lumber**, 0.6 other; orc 11.5: 9.2 / 2.3; undead 6.4 acolytes, 5.1 on gold (27% of states 6-8 at a haunted mine that takes 5) and 0.8 ghouls chopping; night elf 9.3 wisps: 5.6 / 2.8. The busiest mine has more than 5 gold workers in 77% of human and 87% of orc states (8+ in 42% / 67%). In target games (75% of training) the worker count rises to the mask's cap and the extra go to gold too: human 17.8 workers, **12.1 on gold**, 4.5 on lumber (8+ on one mine in 94% of states); orc 17.0, 10.7 / 6.1. So "too many in the goldmine" is human and orc first; undead and night elf can not stack past 5 (their 6th is refused or stands).
- **Supply blocks** (food used at the cap below 100, from minute 2, untaxed traces): policy human 8%, orc 16%, undead 4%, night elf 19% of the game; the AI 1-2%. Lumber was short of a farm in 12% (human), 52% (orc), 71% (undead), 38% (night elf) of the blocked steps; it could have afforded one in 70 / 28 / 3 / 42% (late farms, not only money).
- **Candidly: more lumber alone does not buy wins.** In games of 15+ minutes, the top third by lumber a minute against the bottom third: human 0.56 vs 0.53 won (tier 2+ 0.84 vs 0.16), orc 0.74 vs 0.68, undead **0.39 vs 0.75**, night elf **0.31 vs 0.91** (rungs alike). The high-lumber games are the ones where the rush stalled and it teched; lumber is necessary for the long game, not sufficient.

**What failed before and why.** The oversaturation lesson (`fgself-159`/`160`, `--saturation-lesson`): -17.6 points against insane even with the labels off in the warm-up, a label weighing 9-140x a PPO decision; the log's conclusion was "oversaturation is to be handled by a mask instead (no gold harvest to a mine with five workers)", **never built for human and orc** (bit 64's mine cap covers only haunted and entangled mines). `--worker-value 3` (`fgself-239`): -98. The lumber lesson (`--teach 3`, kept since `fgself-150`) fires only in target games; `--teach 4` would fire all game. The second-base line (`fgself-202`, 223, 254, 256): it expanded but the reserve and workers starved the army in the minutes the AI attacks. Net worth (`fgself-262`): no more tech. The worker cap by mines (`--econ-masks 1`) and the ghoul fix (bit 256, `fgself-249`) were kept: masks have helped where lessons hurt.

**Fixes.**
1. **Mask: the human and orc gold mine takes 5** (`--econ-masks` bit 512). A gold harvest (or right-click) on a mine that already has 5 gold workers (counted by the View's assignment and the mine's hidden workers, 6 with one walking as `teacher.SATURATED`) is masked, as bit 64 does for haunted and entangled mines; and a worker trained at a hall whose mine is full is sent to the tree nearest the hall (the bit-256 kind of rewrite in `play.BCAgent`; check first whether the halls' rally points send new workers to the mine past the mask). *How it avoids the past failures*: no labels, so no distillation pull and nothing in the warm-up; it removes only orders measured to add 0 gold; it touches two races, which are judged apart. Judge against a control by rating and by race (human, orc), and by lumber a minute, choppers in the captured states, supply-blocked share and the lumber bank. Cost: ~half a day with tests, one fork (~27M) and a burst.
2. **Inputs: let it see the economy.** The policy has one per-worker flag ("a worker on lumber") and must count up to 96 rows to see a saturated mine. Add on each own hall's mine row "gold workers / 5", and on the global token "own workers on gold / 10, on lumber / 10, idle / 10" and "gold and lumber gathered in the last 60 s / 1000" (zero-initialised columns, as F 28 -> 29). *Avoids*: changes what it sees, pulls on no order. Cost: ~1 day and a run.
3. **Reward, cheap, after 1-2: lumber's float penalty.** The potential counts the bank at -0.5 a point for lumber as for gold, and a tier-1 build has little to spend lumber on, so every chop reads negative until a farm or a keep is bought. `--float-penalty-lumber 0.1` (gold stays 0.5). `fgself-43` (0.2) and `71` (0) changed gold too. Risk: hoarding; watch the bank. Cost: 1-2 hours.

**Logging that would help:** each side's workers by job per minute in the trace (gold per mine, lumber, building, idle; the View has the agent's, `late._role` the AI's). The AI's roles from order and place are unreliable for human (4.8 of 10.4 workers read "other").

## 2. Heroes: one, under-levelled, dies first

**Evidence** (traces; a dead hero stays in the list with alive 0 until revived).
- **Deaths**: 0.61 a game in untaxed wins, **2.88 in untaxed losses**, 2.03 in rung losses; the AI loses 2.96 a game in the policy's wins (the rush kills its hero). The game's first hero death is the policy's in 49 of 58 losses (84%) and 28 of 103 wins (27%). After its hero is trained, it is dead **31% (untaxed) / 36% (rungs) of the time in losses**, 8% in wins; at minute 15 of a loss its only hero is dead in 43-48% of games.
- **Level at death** in losses: level 1 in 57 of 141, 2 in 33, 3 in 27: it dies before it levels, feeding the AI experience.
- **Revival works mostly**: dead a median 94-113 s; the revival's gold on hand a median 16-26 s after the death, the rest the game's revival time. 56 of 141 deaths in losses were never revived, 17 of those with 3+ minutes left (median 5), 13 of which never had the gold.
- **One hero, always**: 2+ heroes in 5 of 196 untaxed long games, 0 of 399 at the rungs, 0.7% of `fgself-267`'s 1,812 no-target training games against the AI; the teacher's tavern-hero lesson fired 0.01 a game there. A second hero needs a tier-2 hall in 1.29, so this is section 1's lumber again. Levels in losses: 2.5 at minute 10, 3.4 at 15, 4.0 at 20; the AI's level sum 4.1, 6.9, 9.6 (2.4 heroes). In wins the policy's hero is level 4 at minute 10 from kills alone.
- **First hero**: 139-159 s by race to the AI's 121-138 s.
- **Candidly**: deaths come late in losses (median 5.1 min before the end) and an early death (before minute 10) costs only 0.81 vs 0.87 won (untaxed, n=50) and 0.16 vs 0.23 (rungs, n=56) in games of 12+ minutes. Much of the dying is the collapse itself; the trace can not say why the hero dies.

**What failed before and why.** Hero levels in the shaping (`--hero-level-value`, `fgself-41`, `64`): no gain, slightly worse. Experience in the shaping (`--hero-xp-value 0.5`, `fgself-242` -69, `248` -83): the likely cost of both, never run alone. The revival lesson ran only in `fgself-157`'s bundle of five lessons (-28; frequent new lessons pull the policy off). The revival's reserve and price fixes (bit 2) are in the recipe. The first-hero weights (`fgself-236`) were kept.

**Fixes.**
1. **Log first** (1-2 hours): each hero death in the episode row: minute, level, its hit points over the last 10 s, own and enemy army value within 1,000, the nearest enemy tower, distance to both bases, the killer's type, its last three orders, a usable town portal held. Without it a fix is aimed blind.
2. **An escape rule** (`--hero-escape`, a harness rule like the burrow stand-down and the auto skills of `--macros 3`, which were kept): a hero below 25% of its hit points whose local fight is lost (the teacher's Lanchester estimate: the enemy 1.5x its side within 900) reads its town portal (held in 96% of games, used 0.07 a game) to its base. *Avoids*: no labels; gated on a measured strength margin so a won fight is not left; once per scroll. Judge by deaths a game, the dead share and rating by race. Cost: ~1 day with a probe. Aim it with fix 1's data first.
3. **No new hero reward or lesson.** The second hero follows tier 2: measure heroes a game after section 1's fixes before anything else.

## 3. No creeping

**Evidence.** Creeps killed a game (1,583 untaxed plateau games): human 0.47, orc 0.57, undead 0.59, night elf 0.97 (any in 18-37%, 3+ in 12-20%); the AI against it 1.9-4.3, and 16.9 in its wins (0.53 a minute in 20-minute games to the policy's 0.05). In losses the AI's heroes together are 3.4 levels ahead at minute 15 (6.9 to 3.4) and its items are mostly camp drops (ring of protection, claws, gloves, circlet: section 5). Long games with 3+ creeps against none: untaxed 15-20 min 0.98 vs 0.86, 20+ min 0.35 vs 0.26; rungs 0.61 vs 0.22 and 0.35 vs 0.11. Confounded: it creeps when it is safe or ahead.

**What failed before and why.** `fgself-242`: experience paid, creeping did not rise (a reward cannot pay for what never happens), -69. `fgself-245`: the lesson uncapped crept 4-5 a game and took the army over (0.10 against insane). `246`-`248`: the place loss pulled every attack-move to camp cells, then the order loss pushed attack-moves (1.4-3.3 a step); floored on the place alone (`248`) it was healthy but the creeping faded, -83 (and the cap never reached the games: bug finder 11). `fgself-257`, capped as meant: faded, -33 ("uncapped it takes the army over, capped it fades"; its late starts confounded, bug finder 12). The camp lesson's raid guard was fixed 2026-11-27 (audit 21). **No more creep lessons.**

**Why it may not creep, beyond exploration.** The shaping counts units' cost times hit points: every hit a creep lands on the hero or army is a material loss at once, while the experience and the drop count nothing. And the camps out of sight are not in its view at all: it can not point at a camp it has not stumbled on.

**Fixes.**
1. **The camps as map knowledge (input).** Each of Echo Isles' camps as a remembered neutral row, as `--inputs 3` keeps enemies it saw: position, its creeps' total level, cleared or not as last seen, the level of its drop. People know the camps from the map, as the hunt lesson already knows the mines' places. *Avoids*: no labels, no place or order pull; it can only make a camp a target the policy chooses. Judge by creeps a game without lessons, hero level at 10 minutes, rating by race. Cost: ~1.5 days (the camps from the first step's neutral units, zero-initialised rows).
2. **Then, if creeping still never starts, a reward that was never run alone:** experience at a low weight (`--hero-xp-value 0.1`, a camp's ~250 experience read as ~25 material), alone, with nothing else changed. 242 and 248 both mixed it with other changes. Cheap (the flag exists); stop at the first sign of 242's drop.

## 4. Tech and army: one unit, no upgrades, no anti-air

**Evidence.** In losses research 1.5 levels to the AI's 16.6, tier 1.5 to 2.8. Armies in 15+ minute games: human 98% footmen, undead 100% ghouls, night elf 100% archers, orc 59% headhunters and 36% grunts. Against 4+ AI air units: human 0/5, orc 0/6, undead 0/15, night elf 0/12 won (night elf's archers hit air and lost too: the air marks an out-teched game, not only a missing counter). Its own tier 2+ in long games: untaxed 0.62 vs 0.67 won (no help), rungs 0.51 vs 0.16.

**What failed before.** The capped army lesson (`fgself-226`): level, rare (0.23 a game). The capped upgrade lesson (`227`): research rose in training, not in evaluation; level. Research at cost (`--research-value 1`, `213`): -11. Twice the shaping (`264`): develops more, loses more (-74). Late starts from lost positions (`230`) and a learner tax (`259`): level. Targets (75% of training games) teach variety that does not carry over to games without one.

**Fixes.** None new before section 1 lands: the keep costs 210 lumber and every upgrade costs lumber, and the policy chops ~1 worker's worth. After it, re-measure research, tier and the army mix. One diagnostic to add meanwhile: its anti-air share once enemy air is seen, in the episode row.

## 5. Items rarely bought

**Evidence.** Purchases a game: 0.29 in untaxed wins, 0.18 in losses (any in 20% / 10%); the race shop 0.01 a game. The goblin merchant's: dust 61, town portal 48, staff of teleportation 31, invisibility 25, circlet 23 (996 games). The race shop is built by human 5%, orc 2%, undead 1%, night elf 81% of games (and it buys 0.05 items a game there); the AI builds its shop as orc 97%, night elf 100%, undead 49%, human 13%. Held by the policy's heroes: 1.0-1.3 items at minutes 5-20, the free town portal in 96%; the AI's: 1.1, 1.6, 2.6, 6.7 at 5, 10, 15, 20 minutes (rungs 1.7, 3.1, 4.4, 5.5). Used up (gone from a living hero): the policy 0.13 a game, the AI 1.53 (lesser clarity 56, salves 45, healing 19 and mana potions 19 in 98 games), 2.29 at the rungs. The teacher's potion and town-portal lessons fired 0.00 a game in no-target training games against the AI (it holds no potions). Long games with a purchase: 0.74 vs 0.64 won untaxed, 0.37 vs 0.20 at the rungs (confounded).

**Two structural reasons.** (a) Items are not in the shaping's material (units and buildings only): a purchase reads as the bank's penalty lifted, +0.5 of its cost, where a unit reads +1.5; only research is treated as badly. (b) Buying takes a hero's walk to a shop it rarely builds. In its favour: an agent slot is a computer slot and the engine has its heroes drink potions and salves by themselves in a fight (`shopitems.py`), so a potion bought is a potion used.

**What failed before.** `--shop-items 1` (`fgself-179`): kept, it learned to leave the new orders alone (0.06 bought a game). `--z-shop 3` (`183`): bought 3.2 a game, mostly the wrong things early (dust, rods), the gold taken from the army, -22. The potion lesson's misuse mid-fight was fixed in audit 21.

**Fixes.**
1. **Items in the material, at their cost** while held (consumables until used, then their effect shows in hit points). *Avoids*: no targets or labels (183's pull), no forced purchase; the potential only stops calling a purchase a loss. Caveat: research at cost (213) was -11; judge by rating and purchases a game. Cost: ~half a day (the C material helper reads the item columns 17-22 or a Python add-on).
2. **Log the opportunity first** (1 hour): steps where a hero stands within 1,500 of its shop or the merchant with a free slot and the bank above the cheapest healing item, to tell money from will.
3. Creeping (section 3) brings the AI's permanent items; it is the larger part of the items gap.

## Confounds and limits

- Most comparisons between won and lost games are associations: long games are lost and are also where the AI techs, creeps and buys, so "X vs not" splits overstate X. Where a within-length split exists it is given; the lumber split shows the trap (the high-lumber games are lost more).
- The AI plays at its double income: its gold and lumber a minute are about twice a person's; the gap that matters is lumber (2-7x after halving), not gold.
- Traces are few (8 untaxed losses); the hero and supply numbers lean on the rung games (58 losses), where the agent's income is taxed.
- The captured states come from training games (82% with a target, curriculum at its floor); no-target states are the closer match to evaluation and are given apart.
- The .w3g replays hold no agent orders (the map script issues them), and the commands' unit ids need a playback to tell a mine from a hall: the replays can not show worker assignment without the game. The captured states do.

## Recommended order (one change a run, each against a control by rating and by race)

1. **The human/orc gold-mine mask** (section 1, fix 1), then **the economy inputs** (fix 2). Cheapest, upstream, the class of change that has helped.
2. **Hero-death logging** now (hours), then **the escape rule** aimed by it.
3. **The camps as map knowledge** (section 3, fix 1).

After these, the cheap reward tests in this order: lumber's float penalty, items at cost, experience at 0.1 alone. No new lessons.
